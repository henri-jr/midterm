A Room with a View

By E. M. Forster — Part I, Chapter I: The Bertolini

"The sign said 'No Italians Admitted'—or rather, 'Si si, piacerà se potrete,' which being interpreted means..." A quiet beginning in a Florentine pensione.

Scene I: The Disappointment at Lunch

"The river is winding and delicious," said Miss Bartlett, adjusting her veil. "It is a thousand pities we have not got it from our windows. But I am sure I cannot blame Signora Bertolini."

Lucy Honeychurch waved her hand in polite agreement, though her heart felt a slight pang of disappointment over the modest view they had been assigned.

Scene II: The Encounter with Mr. Emerson

At the far end of the dining table sat Mr. Emerson and his son George, two Englishmen who appeared to operate entirely outside the rigid social codes of the pension.

The elder Mr. Emerson abruptly disrupted the polite hum of conversation by offering to exchange rooms—insisting that Lucy and her cousin take their rooms with a view instead.

Scene III: The Ripple of Convention

A heavy, scandalized silence fell across the other guests. Miss Bartlett stiffened, immediately perceiving an improper overture in the kind gesture.

Yet, as Lucy looked from her cousin's strained expression out toward the sunlit Arno, she realized that ordinary life would never quite feel the same again.
